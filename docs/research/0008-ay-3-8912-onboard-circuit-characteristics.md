# Does the ZX Spectrum 128K/+2/+3's on-board circuit around the AY-3-8912 chip filter or shape its audio output, the way the 48K beeper's output stage does?

<!-- Confidence levels and what counts as research are in ../../DOC-MAP.md. -->

**Confidence:** medium for the AY-3-8912 chip's own output characteristics (a primary datasheet was
read directly); medium for the on-board amplifier circuit's topology and its well-documented
hardware bug (two real schematic diagrams were read directly, corroborated by an independent
restoration article, though both schematics are cropped and neither was traced as a full netlist);
low for concluding that this circuit has no bearing on `PLAY`/`APLAY`'s output authenticity, since
that conclusion rests on interpreting a photographed/redrawn schematic rather than a measured
frequency response.

## Finding

The AY-3-8912 chip itself has no on-die filtering: its datasheet describes each channel's output pin
as a plain 4-bit logarithmic D/A converter producing "an up to 1V peak-peak signal representing the
complex sound waveshape generated by the PSG," with no recommended external filter network given.
This matches how BazLang's `VoiceFrame`/`fillPlayMix` already models it - a resolved analogue-ish
level per channel, not a signal needing edge-smoothing the way the 48K beeper's single toggled bit
does (see [`docs/research/0007`](0007-zx-spectrum-beeper-output-smoothing.md)).

The ZX Spectrum 128K family's own board, however, does put real circuitry between the AY chip and
the final audio output: two cascaded BJT amplifier stages (`TR4`, `TR5`, both BC549) with
AC-coupling capacitors between them (100nF, seen as `C39`/`C40` on the +3 schematic), feeding an
output coupling capacitor (`C38`, 1uF) toward the RF modulator. This is confirmed directly from two
real schematic crops (`+3.png`, the original Amstrad-designed +2A/+3 "Z70830" board; `+3B.png`, the
corrected +2B "Z70833" board) hosted at the source cited below, both read directly as images.

Critically, this circuit is independently and well documented as **broken on the original +2A/+3
board** - not merely non-ideal, but a real design fault that visibly distorts and clips the AY's
audio, fixed in the later +2B revision by changing seven resistor values, adding two more, and
removing a capacitor (`C37`) next to the RF modulator. This is a **bias-point/clipping fault in an
amplifier stage**, not an intentional frequency-shaping filter - the opposite kind of thing from the
beeper's edge-rounding smoothing that
[`docs/research/0007`](0007-zx-spectrum-beeper-output-smoothing.md) is about. The AC-coupling
capacitors visible in both schematics (100nF inter-stage, 1uF to the modulator) are consistent with
ordinary DC-blocking between amplifier stages, not a deliberate treble-rolloff/smoothing network; a
coupling capacitor a few hundred nF into a few kOhm rolls off _bass_, not the sharp edges a
beeper-style smoothing filter would round off. This finding therefore does not support adding a
beeper-style edge-smoothing filter to `PLAY`/`APLAY`'s AY-modelled output - if anything, it suggests
the "well-known 128K/+3 sound problem" real collectors and restorers refer to is a
specific-hardware-revision clipping bug that is arguably a `docs/quirks.md`-style accepted oddity of
certain boards, not a universal characteristic worth deliberately reproducing.

## Evidence

- <https://archive.org/stream/General_Instrument_AY-3-8910/General_Instrument_AY-3-8910_djvu.txt> -
  the manufacturer's own datasheet. States "Each of these signals is the output of its corresponding
  D/A Converter, and provides an up to 1V peak-peak signal representing the complex sound waveshape
  generated by the PSG," and that "the analog sound outputs can each provide 4 bits of logarithmic
  digital to analog conversion." No external filter/load-resistor/op-amp circuit is recommended
  anywhere in the datasheet text checked.
- `http://zxnet.co.uk/spectrum/+3sound/` (linked as the original source from the
  spectrumforeveryone.com article below) - hosts the two schematic images this finding is based on
  (`+3.png`, `+3B.png`), read directly here rather than through a text summary. States plainly: "The
  Spectrum +2A/+3 motherboard (Amstrad Z70830) has a notoriously broken amplification circuit which
  heavily distorts audio output," and gives the fix as a resistor-value table (see Evidence below) -
  but its own page text gives no circuit-theory explanation of _why_ the original values cause
  distortion; that has to be read from the schematics themselves.
  - `+3.png` (original, faulty Z70830 board): shows `TR5` (BC549) as a first amplifier stage, biased
    by `R42`/`R43`, with its collector load `R62`; AC-coupled via `C40` (100nF) to a second stage
    `TR4` (BC549) biased by `R63`/`R64`, with collector-side resistors `R65`/`R66`/`R67`; a further
    100nF cap `C39` and a 1uF output capacitor `C38` (with `R72`) lead off toward what the
    associated article identifies as the RF modulator, alongside a small diode cluster (`D36`-`D38`)
    near the 0V rail. A 6MHz crystal (`XL3`) with `C32`/`C33`/`C37` appears on the same crop, likely
    serving clock generation elsewhere on the board rather than the audio signal path itself - not
    fully resolved here (see Open questions).
  - `+3B.png` (corrected Z70833 "+2B" board): the same topology, with revised resistor values at the
    equivalent reference designators (`R205`, `R207`, `R208`, `R201`, `R202`, `R203`, `R200` in
    place of `R62`, `R43`, `R42`, `R63`, `R64`, `R67`, `R72`) and one added resistor (`R206`)
    between the second transistor's bias network and ground that has no counterpart on the original
    board - matching the textual fix instructions below closely enough to confirm the two images are
    a genuine before/after pair for the same real hardware revision.
- <https://www.spectrumforeveryone.com/technical/fixing-audio-distortion-spectrum-3/> - restates the
  same fix as a plain resistor table (all values 0.25W): `R42` 1.2kOhm, `R43` 5.6kOhm, `R62`
  1.5kOhm, `R63` 2.2kOhm, `R64` 6.8kOhm, `R67` 15kOhm, `R72` 820ohm, plus two added resistors not
  present on the original board (`R204` 10kOhm between `TR5`'s base and ground, `R206` 390ohm
  between `TR5`'s emitter and 0V) and removing "ceramic capacitor C37, next to the modulator... for
  best results." Credits the original circuit analysis to its original author, with additional
  assistance credited to a named contributor (see the article for attribution). Provides no
  circuit-theory explanation of the fault itself, only the fix, and includes an audio recording
  (linked from the article, not fetched here) said to demonstrate the difference.
- Search-engine-derived corroboration (not independently re-fetched as primary text): community
  restoration threads and blog posts (sinclairzxworld.com's "AY Sound Levelling Modification"
  thread, retrorepairsandrefurbs.com's +2/+3 restoration write-ups) independently describe the same
  128K/+2/+3 amplifier-circuit fault and near-identical resistor-swap fixes, treating it as common,
  well-known knowledge among Spectrum restorers rather than a one-off claim - the
  sinclairzxworld.com thread itself returned HTTP 403 to automated fetching here and was not read
  directly.

## Dead ends

- <https://www.spectrumforeveryone.com/technical/zx-spectrum-pcb-schematics-layout/> links to a "128
  Issue 6U, analogue and digital side" schematic set for the _48K-derived_ 128K "Toastrack" (the
  original Sinclair-designed 128K, predating the Amstrad +2A/+3 redesign this note's schematics come
  from) - not fetched in image form here, so this note's findings should not be assumed to describe
  the Toastrack's own audio circuit, which may differ from the +2A/+3/+2B one actually examined.
- <https://worldofspectrum.org/ZXSpectrum128+3Manual/chapter8pt30.html> (the official +3 BASIC
  manual's technical reference chapter) confirms the AY-3-8912's clock (1.7734MHz), tone range
  (27Hz-11kHz), and envelope range (0.1Hz-6kHz), but explicitly defers circuit detail to the
  manufacturer's own datasheet and says nothing about the on-board amplifier stage - a dead end for
  this note's specific question, though useful corroboration of the clock/range figures BazLang's
  own `VoiceFrame`/note-DSL model should already agree with.

## Open questions

- What do `TR4`/`TR5`'s exact bias points look like before and after the resistor fix, and does the
  original fault actually cause clipping (as this note assumes from "heavily distorts") or some
  other failure mode (e.g. crossover distortion, oscillation, excessive gain causing hiss)? Neither
  schematic was traced as a full node-by-node netlist here, and no oscilloscope trace or SPICE
  simulation was consulted - "clipping" is inferred from the word "distorts" and the general shape
  of a two-transistor amplifier with an evidently wrong bias network, not confirmed directly.
- Does the crystal/clock circuitry (`XL3`, `C32`, `C33`, `C37` on the +3 schematic) actually feed
  the AY chip's own clock input, or something else on the same cropped schematic sheet unrelated to
  audio? If it is the AY's clock source, it has no bearing on output filtering either way, but this
  was not resolved from the cropped image alone.
- Is the well-documented +2A/+3 amplifier fault present on _every_ +2A/+3 unit, or only some
  production runs/PCB revisions? The sources above describe it as a design-wide fault (fixed in the
  next board revision, +2B), not a component-tolerance issue affecting only some units, but this was
  not cross-checked against a source enumerating PCB revision history.
- This note did not examine whether the original 48K-derived 128K "Toastrack" board (a different,
  Sinclair-designed PCB predating the Amstrad +2A/+3 redesign) has the same or a different AY output
  circuit - see the first Dead end above.
